## Correction

The first synthesis overfit the corpus to the most obvious shared tension: disciplined systems encountering ambiguous phenomena. That reading produced a coherent prototype, but it flattened references whose power comes from incompatible laws:

- maximal ornament rather than restraint;
- polycentric density rather than one focal phenomenon;
- poetic metaphor rather than truthful instrumentation;
- emotional narrative rather than conceptual premise;
- playful celestial diagram rather than epistemic uncertainty;
- speed, impact, and blur rather than still contemplation;
- graffiti accumulation rather than controlled rupture;
- quiet commerce rather than sublimity;
- ecological absorption rather than system-versus-object opposition.

The system should therefore expose **multiple coherent art ecologies**, not search for one definition of art.

## What a biome is

A biome is not a palette, theme, preset, or page template. It is a bundle of mutually supporting laws:

- how space is organized;
- how attention is distributed;
- what materials can exist;
- how light behaves;
- what typography is allowed to do;
- how information is communicated;
- what timescale motion inhabits;
- what the viewer is invited to do;
- what kinds of transitions feel native;
- what visual failures destroy the ecosystem.

Biomes are overlapping memberships. One reference can belong to several. A composition chooses one **host biome** and may graft limited traits from others.

## Twelve primary biomes

### 1. Operational apparatus

**World:** systems, products, surveillance, deployment, classification, and controlled technical action.

**Space:** measured grids, rails, specifications, evidence fields, strong macro/micro scale contrast.

**Material:** manuals, thermal imagery, industrial markings, clean vector geometry, safety color.

**Typography:** neutral sans or mono, alphanumeric identifiers, specifications, compact metadata.

**Time:** scanning, deployment, stepped reveal, tracking, state change.

**Interaction verbs:** inspect, target, compare, deploy, filter, expose.

**References:** `HQwTjnqXUAAj2Wi.jpg`, `HQzuA1TXsAAepO3.jpg`, `HQ0ueUQW0AAOoaA.png`, `HQu41jJbEAA8jQs.jpg`, `HQui8sgXkAAIf3K.jpg`.

**Failure:** fake telemetry, unrelated HUD language, decorative reticles, illegible microtype.

### 2. Manifesto print

**World:** statements, declarations, doubt, protest, design discourse, and public address.

**Space:** frontal typographic wall, stacked language, narrow margins, text as architecture.

**Material:** ink, newsprint, xerox, paper grain, abrasion, registration error.

**Typography:** monumental condensed display, one rhetorical interruption, marginal publication detail.

**Time:** hard cuts, rhythmic sequencing, speech cadence, print jitter.

**Interaction verbs:** read, confront, interrupt, repeat, erase.

**References:** `HQxDhwIaMAAwFWA.png`, `HQxDiTHawAAzcnN.png`, `HJK52r4WoAAIgHS.jpg`, `HPchYHtaUAAEOMz.jpg`.

**Failure:** treating distorted display type as body text or applying damage without rhetorical meaning.

### 3. Deconstructed editorial

**World:** bodies, brands, fashion, ambition, labor, and concepts made spatial.

**Space:** asymmetric columns, cropped figures, modules, type/image masking, editorial pacing.

**Material:** sculpture, photography, paper, cloth, stone, high-contrast monochrome.

**Typography:** one architectural statement, small editorial witness copy, rotated or cropped concepts.

**Time:** sequence through pages or frames, slow image reveal, masked transitions.

**Interaction verbs:** browse, compare, reveal, reframe, sequence.

**References:** `HQ0h-1AbUAAxfc6.jpg`, `HQ0h_1dacAAgII2.jpg`, `HQxnUyOaEAA90g1.jpg`.

**Failure:** a uniform card grid wearing large type, or fashionable cropping that hides required content.

### 4. Quiet object gallery

**World:** ordinary objects made precious through restraint, repetition, and calm commercial order.

**Space:** large white or neutral fields, isolated product/object, partial repetition, generous pauses.

**Material:** packaging, ceramic, glass, paper, quiet photography, restrained 3D objects.

**Typography:** minimal labels, compact commerce or catalogue text, no theatrical hierarchy.

**Time:** stillness, gentle rotation, slow comparison, small state changes.

**Interaction verbs:** inspect, select, compare, collect, purchase.

**References:** `HQui81SWAAEzfqT.jpg`, `HQxnUyOaEAA90g1.jpg`, portions of `HQui8sgXkAAIf3K.jpg`.

**Failure:** adding atmospheric complexity because empty space feels unfinished.

### 5. Ceremonial reliquary

**World:** icons, saints, avatars, relics, jewels, rank, ritual, and charged adornment.

**Space:** axial shrine, nested frames, approximate symmetry disturbed by accumulation, concentrated hierarchy.

**Material:** porcelain skin, gem, metal leaf, lace, beadwork, dark textile, weathered icon surface.

**Typography:** often absent; optional heraldic marks, marginal titles, liturgical or archival metadata.

**Time:** icon stillness, slow shimmer, ritual opening, breath, suspended secondary motion.

**Interaction verbs:** witness, approach, illuminate, open, venerate, equip.

**References:** `HQtwGHfa8AE12Lm.jpg`, `HQ0m0axXMAAoyev.jpg`, `HQxe0BNagAAswVz.jpg`, `HQxYnGta4AApWzm.jpg`.

**Failure:** reducing ornament to decoration, forcing minimalism, or turning slow ceremony into fast spectacle.

### 6. Ecological elegy

**World:** bodies becoming landscape, wetlands, mourning, botanical symbolism, aftermath, and slow material absorption.

**Space:** enclosed habitat, horizontal body or ecological axis, repeated organic motifs, thresholds instead of hard boundaries.

**Material:** water, vegetation, fabric, flesh, mud, oil glaze, marsh darkness.

**Typography:** usually outside the image; optional literary fragments or botanical archive at the margins.

**Time:** drift, saturation, sinking, wilting, decomposition, seasonal change.

**Interaction verbs:** contemplate, remember, discover, linger, adapt.

**References:** `HQpyoXYWAAELSYb.jpg`, portions of `HQxQBoCbIAAPEWY.jpg`, and the ecological register of `HQy2WPxbkAAeJME.jpg`.

**Failure:** gamifying aftermath, making ambiguity explicit, or romanticizing away death and ethical discomfort.

### 7. Oneiric object-poetry

**World:** one impossible conjunction that makes the ordinary metaphysical, intimate, comic, fragile, or mournful.

**Space:** iconic isolation, simple silhouette, large negative field, nested thresholds.

**Material:** handmade marks, plastic, water, moonlight, domestic matter, small symbolic object.

**Typography:** little or none; an optional signature or sparse poetic caption.

**Time:** suspension, sway, slosh, leak, tiny uncertain changes.

**Interaction verbs:** hold, tilt, release, preserve, puncture, wonder.

**References:** `HQxLIVpWQAAMnOu.jpg`, the conceptual reveal in `HQz7jspasAAMr0c.jpg`.

**Failure:** over-explaining the metaphor, adding dashboard data, or replacing tenderness with cinematic spectacle.

### 8. Machine ecology

**World:** infrastructure as habitat, system opacity, maintenance, labor residue, circulation, and entropy.

**Space:** polycentric labyrinth, layered occlusion, trunks and crossings, no privileged focal point.

**Material:** pipe, corrosion, soot, patch, rubble, scan, mineral residue, structural metal.

**Typography:** sparse equipment codes, maintenance history, archive labels, or none.

**Time:** continuous hidden flow, vibration, leakage, repair, decay, geological accumulation.

**Interaction verbs:** traverse, trace, listen, maintain, isolate, annotate.

**References:** `HQxv0IoWAAAduxK.jpg`, `HJOrbNDWcAA-FuS.jpg`, infrastructural aspects of `HQu41jJbEAA8jQs.jpg`.

**Failure:** cleaning the system into a pristine sci-fi dashboard or forcing one focal object onto a network ecology.

### 9. Celestial cartography

**World:** navigable cosmos, ornamental knowledge, planets, trajectories, sigils, correspondence, and exploratory wonder.

**Space:** hub-and-orbit, horizon and celestial anchors, dense center with diffuse perimeter, paths beyond the frame.

**Material:** parchment, print ink, star field, orbital line, atmospheric landscape, symbolic light.

**Typography:** scripts, tiny labels, map notation, poetic fragments, optional pseudo-information.

**Time:** orbit, migration, twinkle, line drawing, slow expansion.

**Interaction verbs:** navigate, orbit, pan, align, chart, discover.

**References:** `HQzB5OfbgAAeJsD.jpg`, `HQ0I9dfX0AA5dEN.jpg`, `HQvL9WjWIAEkSj7.jpg`, `HQxQBoCbIAAPEWY.jpg`.

**Failure:** turning poetic diagrams into literal dashboards or replacing symbolic flatness with generic photorealistic space.

### 10. Post-digital morphology

**World:** bodies, machines, cloth, anatomy, glass, wires, and digital matter becoming one another.

**Space:** floating specimen, cropped body, exploded view, folds, loops, distributed object constellations.

**Material:** translucent mesh, polished armor, cable, fabric, bone, resin, reflective synthetic skin.

**Typography:** small diagnostics, specimen labels, or none; the object carries information through material state.

**Time:** unfold, breathe, morph, assemble, scan, rotate.

**Interaction verbs:** rotate, dissect, reveal, deform, connect, reassemble.

**References:** `HQzEPekXMAEInrp.jpg`, `HQueQZAaAAEgNek.jpg`, `HPy74-SW4AEcCJV.jpg`, `HQu41jJbEAA8jQs.jpg`, `HQui8sgXkAAIf3K.jpg`.

**Failure:** generic glossy 3D, meaningless diagnostic markings, or uniform material polish.

### 11. Kinetic rupture

**World:** bodies or systems transformed by acceleration, fall, procession, release, impact, or disintegration.

**Space:** one strong trajectory, directional asymmetry, concentrated event against quiet field, time compressed into marks.

**Material:** blur, fragment, smoke, train sequence, flock, flare, scraped print, motion residue.

**Typography:** optional route labels or interrupted titles; never the primary continuous reading layer.

**Time:** burst, fall, launch, scatter, procession, inertia.

**Interaction verbs:** scrub, steer, release, accelerate, interrupt, follow.

**References:** `HQxUNCvaMAAqqEd.jpg`, `HQxQBoCbIAAPEWY.jpg`, `HQuOS_tacAADjGc.jpg`, kinetic aspects of `HQzEPekXMAEInrp.jpg`.

**Failure:** animation everywhere, several vectors competing, or motion without a destination.

### 12. Noise commons

**World:** collective identity, graffiti, anti-design, collage, subculture, media accumulation, and contested surfaces.

**Space:** accumulated marks, boundary-breaking media, dense islands, or explosive diagonal events.

**Material:** spray, xerox, photo fragments, ink, scribble, stickers, digital diagrams, overpainting.

**Typography:** heterogeneous voices, hand lettering, tags, cropped statements, fragments.

**Time:** addition, overwrite, erasure, remix, communal accumulation.

**Interaction verbs:** mark, remix, layer, tear, annotate, contribute.

**References:** `HQsDMF1XkAAobX4.jpg`, noisy registers of `HQxDiTHawAAzcnN.png`, `HQvL9WjWIAEkSj7.jpg`, and `HQxUNCvaMAAqqEd.jpg`.

**Failure:** cleaning the surface into one brand voice or treating every mark as a decorative effect.

## Recurring ecotones

Ecotones are stable hybrid environments repeatedly evidenced by the corpus.

### Measured sublime

**Operational apparatus × ceremonial reliquary or ambiguous phenomenon.**

A precise instrument encounters something it cannot fully classify. This remains a useful ecology for `HQ0I9dfX0AA5dEN.jpg`, `HJOrbNDWcAA-FuS.jpg`, and parts of `HQzuA1TXsAAepO3.jpg`. It is no longer the system default.

### Speculative anatomy

**Post-digital morphology × operational apparatus.**

Body or organism is opened, labeled, scanned, or reconstructed. Seen in `HQui8sgXkAAIf3K.jpg`, `HQxe0BNagAAswVz.jpg`, `HQz7jspasAAMr0c.jpg`, and `HQu41jJbEAA8jQs.jpg`.

### Material palimpsest

**Machine ecology or archive × ceremonial apparition.**

Damage, sediment, print history, and erasure become world-building rather than surface treatment. Seen in `HJOrbNDWcAA-FuS.jpg`, `HQxYnGta4AApWzm.jpg`, `HQ0m0axXMAAoyev.jpg`, and `HQvL9WjWIAEkSj7.jpg`.

### Luminous estrangement

**Quiet object/gallery or architecture × overexposure and uncertain scale.**

Ordinary structures become apparitions through light, repetition, absence, or color temperature. Seen most clearly in `HQy2WPxbkAAeJME.jpg`.

## Mixing architecture

### 1. Choose one host biome

The host controls:

- spatial grammar;
- attention hierarchy;
- default density;
- time model;
- viewer relationship;
- frame and passage behavior.

A host is not averaged with another host.

### 2. Graft by channel, not by whole style

A secondary biome contributes through one named channel:

- `material`;
- `light`;
- `typography`;
- `information`;
- `motion`;
- `interaction`;
- `framing`.

Example: an ecological-elegy host may accept operational-apparatus **information** as a botanical archive without adopting a military grid, scan line, and deployment motion system.

### 3. Limit active grafts

Default maximum:

- one host;
- up to two grafts;
- each graft occupies one channel;
- one explicitly declared high-friction contradiction at most.

This prevents “creative” output from becoming a feature average.

### 4. Require a bridge or declare conflict

A hybrid is **symbiotic** when the biomes share a law:

- archive + machine ecology share evidence and layers;
- reliquary + celestial cartography share axis, symbol, and ornament;
- manifesto print + noise commons share public voice and physical mark;
- post-digital morphology + kinetic rupture share transformation and material instability.

A hybrid is **tensional** when the disagreement is the thesis:

- quiet object gallery + noise commons;
- ecological elegy + operational apparatus;
- ceremonial reliquary + manifesto print.

A hybrid is **destructive** when one biome erases the condition that makes the other work:

- dense dashboard grafted onto oneiric object-poetry;
- fast kinetic choreography grafted onto ecological aftermath;
- strict corporate minimalism grafted onto avatar baroque;
- generic neon cyberpunk grafted onto machine ecology’s real material history.

### 5. Preserve the host’s attention economy

A graft cannot introduce a second unrelated focal system unless the composition explicitly becomes polycentric. The host decides whether one focal point is required at all.

### 6. Preserve material causality inside each channel

Texture, annotation, ornament, or motion may be poetic in some biomes and evidentiary in others. The system must not globalize one standard of truth. Celestial cartography may use pseudo-information intentionally; operational apparatus may not.

## Consequences for viewport frames and scroll

The frame architecture must not impose one transition grammar on every biome.

- operational apparatus favors stepped reveal, scan, and precise cuts;
- manifesto print favors hard cuts, typographic replacement, and rhythmic interruption;
- deconstructed editorial favors masking, page succession, and controlled image carry;
- quiet object gallery favors stillness and gentle comparison;
- ceremonial reliquary favors approach, illumination, opening, and long holds;
- ecological elegy favors slow scroll, drift, dark adaptation, and nearly imperceptible change;
- oneiric object-poetry favors tactile physics and one consequential interaction;
- machine ecology favors traversal, persistent flow, and polycentric exploration;
- celestial cartography favors orbit, pan, alignment, and expansion;
- post-digital morphology favors morph, unfold, rotate, and dissection;
- kinetic rupture favors bursts, scrubbed trajectories, and inertia;
- noise commons favors accumulation, overwrite, contribution, and erasure.

Therefore `Frame`, `Passage`, and transition work should become **biome-aware hosts**, not the source of a universal cinematic style.

## Current decision boundary

1. Keep the measured-sublime prototype as evidence for one ecotone.
2. Do not generalize its five-force grammar to all HyvUI composition.
3. Define biome metadata and compatibility before implementing viewport sequences.
4. Prototype radically different host biomes before claiming the architecture is broad:
   - one dense ornamental reliquary;
   - one ecological elegy;
   - one oneiric object-poem;
   - one polycentric machine ecology;
   - one typographic manifesto;
   - one kinetic rupture.
5. Revisit frame and scroll architecture only after those prototypes demonstrate different time and attention laws.
